# Desk research

**Summary.** Secondary research done before any interviews, to sharpen the hypotheses and the guide. It covers the payroll software options an Indian startup can choose from, the statutory payroll items a small employer must handle, and recurring themes in public reviews. It informs the interviews but does not replace them. Nothing here says how startups actually behave. All sources were accessed on 16 September 2026. Prices and rules change; check before reuse.

> This is not legal or tax advice. Where sources disagree or I could not confirm a point, I say so.

**What this suggests (to test, not conclude):**
- **Entry prices are modest.** For a 15-person startup, the listed entry plans above range from ₹1,000 to ₹3,499 a month before GST. Plans differ in scope, so this is not a like-for-like comparison. If price is rarely the barrier, the barriers may be trust, switching effort and the CA relationship. Sections 5 and 6 of the guide probe this.
- **Pricing models differ.** Some charge per organisation with employees included, others add per-employee fees. Buyers may compare "cost at our size", not headline price. Question 6.1 asks what they pay today.

## 2. Statutory payroll items for a small employer

### 2.1 The Labour Codes (status as of September 2026)

- **In force.** The four Labour Codes (Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations, 2020; Social Security, 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions, 2020) took effect on 21 November 2025. They replace 29 central labour laws. ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2192463&reg=3&lang=2); [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas guide](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Guide-to-the-Labour-Codes.pdf))
- **Old rules still apply where not inconsistent.** Until new rules replace them, rules under the repealed laws continue where they do not conflict with the Codes. The notifications gave employers no transition period. ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Guide-to-the-Labour-Codes.pdf))
- **Central rules.** Draft central rules were published around the turn of 2026 ([News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-notifies-draft-rules-for-four-labour-codes/)). Final central rules were notified on 8 May 2026 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-127.html)); one source dates the OSH Code rules to 9 May 2026 ([Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-05-11/economy/centre-notifies-2026-rules-under-the-four-labour-codes-which-came-into-force-in-2025)).
- **State rules vary.** As of May 2026, Karnataka had notified rules under all four Codes. West Bengal had notified none. ([iPleaders, 9 May 2026](https://blog.ipleaders.in/labour-codes-2026-implementation-complete-guide-to-what-is-in-force-what-changed-and-what-to-do-now/)) I could not confirm the position as of September 2026.

**Changes most relevant to payroll** ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Guide-to-the-Labour-Codes.pdf)):
- **The 50% wage rule.** One definition of "wages" applies across the Codes. If excluded components (such as HRA and conveyance) exceed 50% of total pay, the excess counts as wages. This can raise PF, gratuity and other payouts for startups with low basic pay.
- **Wider "employee" definition.** Now includes supervisory, managerial and administrative staff.
- **Fixed-term staff** get gratuity after one year of service.

H6 tests whether startups have acted on any of this.

### 2.2 Recurring monthly and annual items

| Item | Who it applies to | Key figures | Deadline | Source |
|------|------------------|-------------|----------|--------|
| **Provident Fund (PF)** | Establishments with 20 or more employees | 12% of wages from employee and from employer, on a wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month | 15th of the following month | Threshold: [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Guide-to-the-Labour-Codes.pdf). Rates, ceiling and date: [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/provident-fund-pf-due-date) (secondary; EPFO pages could not be accessed) |
| **Employees' State Insurance (ESI)** | Establishments with 10 or more employees; employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month (₹25,000 for persons with disability) | Employer 3.25%, employee 0.75% of wages | Within 15 days of the end of the month | [ESIC coverage](https://esic.gov.in/coverage); [ESIC contribution](https://esic.gov.in/contribution) |
| **Professional Tax (PT)** | Set by each state; not every state levies it | Capped at ₹2,500 per person per year by the Constitution. Karnataka: nil below ₹25,000 a month, then ₹200 a month (₹300 in February). West Bengal: nil up to ₹10,000 a month, rising to ₹200 a month above ₹40,000 | Varies by state. West Bengal sources conflict (monthly vs annual) | [Article 276(2)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1444398/); [Karnataka, Engage HRMS](https://hrengage.ai/compliance/professional-tax/karnataka); [West Bengal, Zoho Payroll Academy](https://www.zoho.com/in/payroll/academy/taxes-and-compliance/professional-tax-rules.html) |
| **TDS on salary** | Employers paying salaries on which income tax is due | From 1 April 2026, section 392 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces section 192. Quarterly statement: Form 138 (was 24Q). Employee certificate: Form 130 (was Form 16) | Quarterly statements due 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May | [ClearTax on section 392](https://cleartax.in/s/section-392-income-tax-act-2025); [TaxGuru on Form 138](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tds-forms-138-140-replace-forms-24q-26q-fy-2026-27.html) |

**Not verified:**
- The monthly TDS deposit date under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Under the old rules it was the 7th of the following month.
- Whether the PF and ESI rates, ceilings and deadlines above have changed under the Social Security Code rules notified in May 2026.
- ClearTax lists the quarterly salary statement as Form 143. TaxGuru and Zoho Payroll's product page say Form 138. I have used Form 138.

**Why this matters for the interviews:** a 25-person startup across two states may handle PF, ESI, two PT regimes and TDS, each with different portals and dates, on top of Labour Code changes. The guide asks who handles each item (2.2) and what went wrong last time (4.1, 4.2).

## 3. Public review themes

**Method.** I read the reviews visible on each product's review page and noted themes that recurred. These are small, self-selected samples. Many reviewers are not Indian startups. Themes are prompts for interview questions, not findings. No quotes are reproduced.

| Product | Page | Rating shown on 16 Sep 2026 | Recurring praise | Recurring complaints |
|---------|------|--------------|------------------|----------------------|
| greytHR | [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/150850/greytHR/reviews/) | 4.3 / 5 (297 reviews) | Ease of use; payroll automation; value for money; breadth of HR modules | Inconsistent or slow support, especially for payroll issues; dated interface; app slowness |
| Keka | [Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/149253/keka) | 4.4 / 5 (91 reviews) | Ease of use; payroll and tax calculation; time saved; mobile app | Slow support and implementation; price relative to value, from a few reviewers |
| Zoho Payroll | [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/186299/Zoho-Payroll/reviews/) | 3.7 / 5 (10 reviews) | Simple interface; automated tax and salary calculation | Slow support; rigid rules, such as exit dates; page slowness |

- **RazorpayX Payroll:** I found no dedicated payroll review page on G2 or Capterra.
- **G2:** blocked automated access, so G2 reviews were not read.

**Patterns across products:**
1. **Ease of use is praised for all three.** It may be the minimum buyers expect, not a reason to choose.
2. **Slow support is a complaint for all three.** This suggests the hard part comes after purchase, during payroll exceptions and implementation. Guide question 5.4 tests it.
3. **Exceptions may be where tools strain.** Zoho Payroll reviewers mention rigid handling of exit dates. One product is thin evidence, but it fits H4's focus on inputs and unusual months. Guide question 3.4 probes it.
