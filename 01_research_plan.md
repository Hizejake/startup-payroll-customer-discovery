# Research plan

**Summary.** I want to learn how Indian startups with 10–150 staff run payroll and statutory compliance today, what it costs them, and what makes them change. I will test six falsifiable hypotheses through 10–12 interviews with founders, HR leads and finance leads. Each hypothesis states in advance what evidence would refute it. Desk research ([05](05_desk_research.md)) shaped the hypotheses; interviews will test them.

## 1. Objective

Answer four questions with evidence from real payroll runs, not opinions:

1. How do startups of different sizes run payroll and compliance today?
2. Where does the time go, and what goes wrong?
3. What event makes a startup change its method or tool?
4. Who decides, and what do they compare?

Out of scope: pricing tests, feature requests, and any product pitch.

## 2. Hypotheses

Each hypothesis maps to a column in [synthesis_tracker.csv](synthesis_tracker.csv). I count interviews, not mentions.

| ID | Hypothesis | Supports if | Refutes if |
|----|-----------|-------------|------------|
| H1 | Startups with 10–24 staff run payroll on spreadsheets plus an external CA, not payroll software. | Most interviewees in the 10–24 band use a spreadsheet as the system of record and a CA for filings. | Half or more in that band use payroll software as the system of record. |
| H2 | The move to payroll software follows a specific event (crossing the PF or ESI threshold, an error or notice, or hiring an HR or finance person), not a gradual rise in effort. | Most interviewees who switched name a trigger event and can date it. | Most switchers describe a gradual build-up and cannot name an event. |
| H3 | In startups under 50 staff, a founder or finance lead owns payroll and approves any tool purchase. HR does not. | In most under-50 interviews, the founder or finance lead approved the last payroll change. | An HR lead made that decision in half or more of them. |
| H4 | Most monthly payroll time goes on collecting inputs (attendance, leave, reimbursements, joiners and leavers), not on calculation or filing. | Most walk-throughs show input collection as the longest step. | Most show calculation, checking or filing as the longest step. |
| H5 | At least half of startups had a payroll or compliance error in the last 6 months: a wrong salary, a late PF, ESI, PT or TDS payment, or a notice. | Half or more report at least one such error. | A quarter or fewer report one. |
| H6 | The Labour Codes, in force since 21 November 2025, have not yet changed how most small startups run payroll. | Most have made no change to salary structures or processes because of them. | Most have restructured salaries or changed processes since November 2025. |

"Most" means more than half of the relevant interviews. Results between "supports" and "refutes" are recorded as unclear, which is a valid finding. Do not change these thresholds after seeing the data.

## 3. Who to interview

**Target profile.** People who ran, checked or approved payroll in the last 3 months at an India-based startup with 10–150 people on payroll.

**Roles.** Founders, HR leads and finance leads. Aim for at least 4 founders and at least 4 HR or finance leads.

**Size bands.** Aim for 4 interviews in each band: 10–24, 25–49 and 50–150 staff. The first band spans the ESI (10) and PF (20) coverage thresholds, where statutory work begins. The 50 cut-off assumes startups hire a dedicated HR person around that size; the interviews will show whether that holds.

**Screen out.**
- Companies under 10 or over 150 people on payroll.
- People with no hands-on or approval role in payroll.
- People who work for, or advise, a payroll or HR software company.
- Practising CAs as primary interviewees. Their view is useful but second-hand. Ask founders for a CA introduction instead, as a follow-up.

Screener questions are in [03_screener_and_outreach.md](03_screener_and_outreach.md).

## 5. Sample size

Plan for 10–12 interviews.

- This is exploratory research. The goal is to find the main patterns and test whether each hypothesis survives, not to measure how common each pattern is.
- In one well-known study, most themes appeared within the first 12 interviews of a fairly uniform group ([Guest, Bunce and Johnson, 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1525822x05279903)).
- This sample is less uniform: three size bands and three roles. With about 4 interviews per band, band-level findings are weak signals only.
- Stop early if interviews 9–12 add nothing new. Add interviews in a band if it is still producing surprises.

## 6. Consent and privacy

- State at the start: who I am, that this is student research, that nothing is being sold, and how the notes will be used.
- Ask before recording. Take notes if the answer is no. Never record silently.
- Do not collect named employees' salaries or personal data. Ask for ranges and processes, not payslips.
- Anonymise before anything is shared. Use interview IDs (P01, P02). Describe companies by size band, sector and city only.
- Keep raw notes and recordings out of this repository. Commit only anonymised, coded data. Delete recordings once notes are checked.
- Offer each interviewee the anonymised summary.
