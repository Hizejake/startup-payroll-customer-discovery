# Interview guide

**Summary.** A 30-minute guide for founders, HR leads and finance leads. It follows The Mom Test ([Fitzpatrick](https://www.momtestbook.com/)): ask about what people did last time, in detail, not what they think or might do. The core is a step-by-step walk-through of the last payroll run. Each question lists probes and the hypothesis it tests (H1–H6, defined in [01_research_plan.md](01_research_plan.md)).

## Rules for the interviewer

1. **Past, not future.** "What happened last month?" beats "What would you want?"
2. **Specifics, not generalities.** When you hear "usually" or "always", ask for the last time.
3. **Listen.** Talk for less than a fifth of the call. Silence is fine; let them fill it.
4. **No pitching.** Do not describe any product. If asked for a recommendation, offer to talk after the interview.
5. **Write their words.** Note short phrases verbatim, in quotation marks. Mark your own interpretations with [me].
6. **Follow the energy.** If a story is rich, drop lower-priority questions. Never drop section 3.
7. **Numbers need a source.** For every figure, ask "How do you know?" Note whether it was checked or guessed.
8. **Probes are options, not a script.** Use them when an answer is vague. Thirty minutes will not fit every probe.

| Section | Minutes | Hypotheses |
|---------|---------|------------|

## 1. Warm-up and consent (3 min)

**Opening script.** "Thanks for your time. I'm a final-year student at Jadavpur University studying how startups run payroll and compliance. I'm not selling anything. I'll keep your company anonymous. Is it OK if I record, just for my notes? No problem if not."

**1.1** What does the company do, and roughly how many people are on payroll this month?
- How many are full-time employees, and how many are contractors, interns or consultants?
- In which states do people work? (Professional tax and some Labour Code rules vary by state.)

**1.2** What is your part in payroll: do you prepare it, check it or approve it?
- How long have you done that?
- Who did it before you?

## 2. Current workflow (5 min)

**2.1** Between the start of the month and salary day, who touches payroll? List everyone, inside and outside the company. *(H1, H3)*
- What exactly does the CA or accountant do? What do they not do?
- How is the CA paid: a fixed monthly fee, per filing, or as part of a wider retainer?
- Who has the final say before money leaves the bank?

**2.2** Which statutory payments does the company make today: PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS on salary? Who handles each one? *(H1)*
- When did you register for PF and for ESI? What prompted it?
- Is any of it done by the CA's team rather than yours?

## 3. Last payroll run, step by step (8 min)

The most important section. Get one real run in sequence. Do not accept a description of the "usual" process.

**3.1** Let's take last month's payroll. When did work on it start, and what was the very first thing that happened? *(H4)*
- Where did attendance and leave data come from? Who sent it, and in what form?
- How did you find out about joiners, leavers and salary changes?
- What about reimbursements, bonuses or deductions?

**3.2** What happened next? Keep going until salaries reached people's accounts. *(H4)*
- How long did that step take?
- Who checked the numbers? What were they looking for?
- How was salary paid: bank upload file, payroll tool or one transfer at a time?
- Did any step wait on someone else? For how long?

**3.3** Which step took longest? Which step do you worry about most? *(H4)*
- Are those the same step? If not, why not?

**3.4** Was anything about that month unusual: a leaver's final settlement, arrears, a bonus, a new state?
- If it was a normal month, when was the last unusual one? What changed that time?

**3.5** After salaries, what happened with PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS that month? *(H4, H5)*
- Who paid and filed each? On what date, roughly?
- Did the CA or anyone else have to chase you for information?

## 4. Pain and its cost (6 min)

**4.1** Tell me about the last time payroll or compliance went wrong. *(H5)*
- When was it? What happened?
- Who noticed first: you, an employee, the CA, or a government portal?
- How was it fixed, and how long did the fix take?
- Did it cost money: interest, a late fee, a penalty? How much?
- What did you change afterwards, if anything?

**4.2** In the last 6 months, has the company had a notice, late fee or interest charge on PF, ESI, professional tax or TDS? *(H5)*
- If yes: what was it for, and how was it resolved?
- If no: how confident are you? Who would know for sure?

**4.3** Roughly how many hours did payroll and compliance take last month, for you and for everyone else? *(H4)*
- How did you arrive at that figure?
- How does a bad month compare?
- What did that time take you away from?

**4.4** What have you already tried to make payroll easier or safer? *(H1, H2)*
- What happened with that?
- If nothing: why not? (Doing nothing is a signal. The pain may be tolerable.)

**4.5** Since November 2025, has the company changed salary structures or payroll processes because of the new Labour Codes? *(H6)*
- If yes: what changed, who drove it, and what did it cost in time or money?
- If no: has anyone, such as the CA, raised it? What did you decide?
- Do not explain the Codes. If they have not heard of them, note that and move on.

## 5. Tools: choosing and switching (5 min)

**5.1** What do you use for payroll today? Include spreadsheets, the CA's software and bank portals. *(H1)*
- What is the system of record: where would you look to find what someone was paid in March?
- What do you still do outside the tool? *(H4)*

**5.2** Take me back to when you started using your current method or tool. What was going on at the time? *(H2)*
- What were you doing before?
- What happened that made you look for something new? When was that?
- Which other options did you look at? Why did you rule them out?
- Who else was involved in the choice?
- How long from first looking to the first live payroll run?

**5.3** Have you ever stopped using a payroll tool, a payroll service or a CA? What happened? *(H2)*
- What was the final straw?
- What did switching cost you in time, data clean-up or errors?

**5.4** Think of the last time you needed help with payroll, from the tool's support team or the CA. What happened? *(tests the support theme in [05_desk_research.md](05_desk_research.md))*
- How long until it was fixed?
- What did you do while you waited?

## 6. Budget, decision and close (3 min)

**6.1** What does payroll cost you today, all in: software, CA fees and staff time?
- Is the software priced per employee or as a flat fee?
- Which budget does it come from?

**6.2** The last time you bought or changed a payroll tool or service, who approved it? *(H3)*
- Who could have said no?
- Did anyone outside the company, such as the CA or an investor, influence the choice?

**6.3** Close.
- "Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?"
- "Who else should I speak to? Would you introduce me to your CA or to another founder?"
- "May I send you the anonymised findings?"

An introduction or a follow-up offer is a stronger signal of interest than praise. Record which one you got.

## Variants by role

**Founders**
- They often know the decisions, not the steps. In section 3, ask "Who could walk me through it?" and request an introduction.
- Spend more time on 4.1, 5.2 and 6.2: incidents, the switching story, and approval.
- Ask what they check before approving payroll, and why. This shows what they trust and what they fear.
- Watch for over-confidence: "It's all handled" can mean "the CA handles it and I don't look".

**HR leads and finance leads**
- They know the steps. Give section 3 the full 8 minutes, or more.
- Ask what the founder checks and questions. This reveals approval friction.
- On budget (6.1, 6.2), ask how the last purchase was approved, not "Do you have budget?". Do not put them on the spot about authority.
- Ask who covers payroll when they are on leave. It shows how fragile the process is.

**Company under 20 staff**
- PF may not apply yet. Ask whether they have thought about crossing 20, and whether anyone told them what changes.

## Questions not to ask, and why

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|-----------|-----|-------------|
| "Would you use a tool that automates payroll?" | Hypothetical. People say yes to be polite. | "What have you already tried to make it easier?" |
| "How much would you pay for that?" | Guesses about future spending are unreliable. | "What does payroll cost you today, all in?" |
| "Is payroll painful for you?" | Leading, and a yes/no answer tells you nothing. | "Tell me about the last time it went wrong." |
| "What features would you want?" | People describe solutions, not problems. | "Which step took longest last month?" |
| "What do you think of Keka / greytHR / Zoho / RazorpayX / Asanify?" | Invites brand opinions, not behaviour. It also sounds like a pitch. | "How did you choose what you use now?" |
| "Do you find compliance confusing?" | Invites a general complaint with no detail. | "Who handled last month's PF and TDS, and when?" |
| "What is X's salary?" | Personal data. Unnecessary for this research. | Ask about process and ranges only. |
| "Are you aware of the Labour Codes?" | Tests knowledge and can embarrass. | "Since November 2025, has anything changed because of them?" |
