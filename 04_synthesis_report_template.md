## How to synthesise

### Affinity mapping (about 2 hours for 12 interviews)

1. **One observation per card.** Go through each notes file. Write one fact, quote or behaviour per card (a sticky note, or a spreadsheet row). Tag it with the interview ID.
2. **Separate behaviour from opinion.** Colour-code: what they did, what they said they feel, and your interpretation.
3. **Cluster from the bottom up.** Group cards that feel related without using the hypotheses as headings. Let the groups emerge.
4. **Name each cluster last.** The name should be a claim ("Leavers' final settlements cause most errors"), not a topic ("Errors").
5. **Count interviews, not cards.** Five cards from one talkative founder are one interview.
6. **Map back.** Only now compare clusters with H1–H6. Clusters that match no hypothesis are often the best insights.

### Avoid overclaiming from 12 interviews

- **Use counts, not percentages.** Write "7 of 12", never "58%". Percentages imply a representative sample.
- **Say what exists, not how common it is.** Twelve interviews show which patterns are real and why they happen. They do not show market share.
- **Flag thin segments.** Any claim about a group of fewer than 3 interviews is a lead to test, not a finding.
- **Name the bias.** People who reply to a student researcher may be more organised, or more frustrated, than average. Warm introductions cluster similar people.
- **Weight behaviour over opinion.** A penalty they paid outweighs a complaint. A tool they abandoned outweighs a wish list.
- **Label self-reported numbers.** Hours and error counts are estimates unless checked. Report them as ranges.
- **Report disconfirming evidence.** A refuted hypothesis is a useful result, not a failure.
